# Sri Lanka Salary Calculator

Work out what actually reaches your bank account each month: the **Advance Personal Income Tax
(APIT)** deducted by your employer, the **EPF employee contribution** of 8%, and the resulting
**take-home salary**.

## What you enter

| Field | What to put in it |
| --- | --- |
| Basic salary | Your monthly basic salary — the figure EPF is calculated on |
| Cash allowances | Allowances paid to you in cash each month |
| Other taxable income | Non-cash benefits and other taxable employment income |

Three options adjust how the figures are treated:

- **Deduct EPF employee contribution (8%)** — untick if EPF does not apply to you.
- **Apply EPF on allowances too** — tick if your employer calculates EPF on salary *and*
  allowances rather than basic salary alone.
- **Other income is paid in cash** — tick when the extra income actually reaches you as money.
  Leave it unticked for non-cash benefits, which are taxed but never paid out.

### What counts as "other taxable income"

Benefits your employer provides instead of cash still form part of your taxable employment
income: a company vehicle, employer-provided housing, fuel allowances, reimbursed personal
expenses, employer-paid utility bills, and similar perquisites. They increase the tax you pay
without increasing the cash you receive — which is why the calculator keeps them out of the
take-home figure unless you say they are paid in cash.

## APIT tax slabs

The rates below apply from **01 April 2025**. Sri Lanka's personal income tax is progressive:
each slice of your income is taxed at its own rate, so a higher rate never applies to your
whole salary — only to the portion falling inside that band.

| Monthly income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| First Rs. 150,000.00 | 0% |
| Next Rs. 83,333.33 | 6% |
| Next Rs. 41,666.67 | 18% |
| Next Rs. 41,666.67 | 24% |
| Next Rs. 41,666.67 | 30% |
| Balance | 36% |

The first Rs. 150,000 a month (Rs. 1,800,000 a year) is tax-free relief. If your total taxable
income stays under that, no APIT is deducted at all.

## EPF and ETF

| Contribution | Rate | Paid by | Deducted from your salary? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EPF employee | 8% | You | Yes |
| EPF employer | 12% | Your employer | No |
| ETF | 3% | Your employer | No |

A total of 20% of your EPF-applicable earnings goes into your Employees' Provident Fund
account each month, but only the 8% employee share is taken out of your pay. The employer's
12% EPF and 3% ETF are additional costs to your employer, shown in the breakdown so you can
see the full value of your package.

## How your take-home is worked out

1. **Total taxable income** = basic salary + cash allowances + other taxable income.
2. **APIT** is charged on that total using the slabs above. The 8% EPF contribution is *not*
   deductible for tax purposes, so tax is calculated on the gross figure.
3. **EPF (8%)** is deducted from your basic salary — plus allowances, if that option is ticked.
4. **Take-home** = the cash you were paid − APIT − EPF. Non-cash benefits are excluded, since
   they were never paid to you in cash.

The calculator also shows your **effective tax rate** (total tax as a share of your income) and
your **marginal tax rate** (the rate charged on your next rupee earned). The effective rate is
always the lower of the two, because the earlier slabs are taxed more lightly.

## Example

A monthly salary of Rs. 300,000 with no allowances or other income:

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total taxable income | Rs. 300,000.00 |
| APIT | Rs. 18,500.00 |
| EPF employee (8%) | Rs. 24,000.00 |
| **Take-home salary** | **Rs. 257,500.00** |
| Effective tax rate | 6.17% |
| Marginal tax rate | 24% |

Of that Rs. 300,000, the first Rs. 150,000 is tax-free, the next Rs. 83,333.33 is taxed at 6%,
the following Rs. 41,666.67 at 18%, and the remaining Rs. 25,000 at 24%.

## Disclaimer

These figures are indicative and intended as a guide only. Your actual deduction can differ
because of tax credits, multiple sources of employment income, deductions claimed through your
employer, or a change in the rates. Confirm your position with the Inland Revenue Department or
your payroll department.
